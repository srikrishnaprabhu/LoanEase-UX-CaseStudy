## Purpose

This document records the research approach used during the LoanEase project and explains how the research methods contributed to understanding the problem.

Because this documentation was not maintained as a detailed live planning record during the original research phase, parts of this document are reconstructed from the completed research materials.

---

## Research Objectives

The research aimed to understand:

- Users' experiences with loan applications.
- Common pain points.
- Difficult stages of the application process.
- Expectations from digital loan platforms.
- Concerns around documentation and financial information.
- Importance of application status updates.
- Trust when sharing financial documents.
- Features users would value in a digital loan platform.

---

## Research Approach

A mixed-method approach was used.

### Quantitative

An online survey was used to identify:

- Common pain points.
- User expectations.
- Feature preferences.
- Importance of status updates.
- Confidence in digital document submission.
- Future adoption intent.

### Qualitative

Semi-structured interviews were used to understand:

- Personal experiences.
- Reasons behind reported problems.
- Communication issues.
- Documentation challenges.
- Transparency concerns.
- Context surrounding loan application experiences.

---

## Research Methods

| Method | Purpose | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Online survey | Identify patterns across participants | 42 responses; 41 analysed |
| Semi-structured interviews | Explore experiences in greater depth | 12 participants |
| Observation | Understand behaviour in context | Not conducted |
| Quantitative analysis | Analyse survey patterns | Completed |
| Qualitative thematic analysis | Identify recurring interview themes | Completed |

---

## Observation Limitation

Observation was not conducted.

The research involved financial applications and potentially sensitive personal banking activity. Observing users during real financial transactions could create privacy and ethical concerns.

Instead, survey and interview methods were used to collect participant experiences without requiring observation of private banking activity.

This is recorded as a research limitation rather than treated as completed research.

---

## Participant Overview

### Survey

- 42 responses collected.
- 41 valid responses analysed.
- One response excluded because participant consent was not provided.

### Interviews

- 12 semi-structured interviews.
- Participants represented multiple occupations and loan experiences.

---

## Research Analysis

### Survey

Survey analysis considered:

- Participant profile.
- Previous loan experience.
- Loan types.
- Application channels.
- Satisfaction.
- Pain points.
- Difficult application stages.
- Desired features.
- Importance of real-time updates.
- Confidence in document uploads.
- Future adoption intent.

### Interviews

Interview responses were analysed thematically.

Recurring themes included:

- Approval delays.
- Lack of updates.
- Eligibility confusion.
- Documentation challenges.
- Lack of transparency.
- Communication issues.
- Loan terminology and interest-rate confusion.

---

## Research Ethics and Privacy

The project avoided publishing raw survey and interview response data in the public GitHub repository.

Raw response files remain local and are ignored by Git.

The public project uses summarized and analysed findings instead.

---

## Expected Research Output

The research was intended to produce:

- Key findings.
- User pain points.
- User needs.
- Research-backed design opportunities.
- Inputs for personas and user journeys.
- Evidence for subsequent design decisions.

---

## Research Limitations

- Survey sample size was limited to 41 valid responses.
- Twelve interviews were conducted.
- Participants may not represent all geographic regions or banking experiences.
- Observation was not conducted.
- The research reflects experiences during the project period.
