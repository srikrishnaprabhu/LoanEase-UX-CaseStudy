## Purpose

This retrospective records what happened during the LoanEase project, what was learned, what worked, what did not work, and how the experience should influence the remaining portfolio case study.

The retrospective is based on available project documentation, research evidence, assignment material, and Git/GitHub project history.

Where information has been reconstructed after the original activity, this is explicitly identified.

---

# 1. Project Context

LoanEase began as a university UX assignment focused on simplifying digital loan applications.

The original assignment required:

- User research summary.
- Two personas.
- One user journey map.
- Interaction design.
- High-fidelity prototype.
- Team contribution documentation.

The project was subsequently developed as a broader portfolio UX case study.

The portfolio objective is to demonstrate the reasoning and process behind the UX work rather than only presenting the final interface.

---

# 2. What Was Done

The project completed the following major activities:

- Project initiation.
- Problem definition.
- Background research.
- Research planning.
- Online survey.
- Survey analysis.
- Semi-structured interviews.
- Interview analysis.
- Final research report.
- Research handover.
- Project documentation setup.
- Git/GitHub learning and repository organization.
- Repository cleanup.
- Removal of raw research data from public GitHub history.

The next stages are research synthesis, personas, journeys/flows, design, prototyping, usability testing, iteration, and final case-study preparation.

---

# 3. Research Approach

A mixed-method approach was used.

### Survey

42 responses were collected.

41 valid responses were analysed after excluding one response because participant consent was not provided.

### Interviews

12 semi-structured interviews were conducted.

### Observation

Observation was not conducted.

The reason was the financial and privacy-sensitive nature of the project. Observing real financial applications or personal banking activity could expose sensitive information and create ethical concerns.

---

# 4. What the Research Revealed

The survey identified several major challenges:

- Lengthy approval process.
- Difficulty understanding eligibility criteria.
- Lack of application status updates.
- Collecting required documents.
- Poor customer support.
- Technical issues.
- Hidden charges or unclear fees.
- Difficulty understanding loan terms.

The interviews reinforced these findings and added contextual detail around:

- Approval delays.
- Communication problems.
- Documentation challenges.
- Transparency.
- Eligibility.
- Interest rates and charges.
- Inconsistent information across channels.

---

# 5. Important Research Insight

The research suggests that users are not necessarily opposed to digital loan services.

36 of 41 valid survey respondents indicated that they would consider using a well-designed digital loan platform in the future.

This suggests that the primary opportunity is to improve confidence, transparency, communication, and usability rather than simply encouraging digital adoption.

---

# 6. Strongest Design Opportunity Identified

Real-time application status tracking was the most requested feature in the survey, selected by 27 participants.

This aligns with:

- Long approval times.
- Lack of status updates.
- Uncertainty during the waiting stage.
- Interview findings around communication.

This creates a strong research-to-design connection that should be explored during the next phase.

---

# 7. What Worked

## Mixed-Method Research

Using both survey and interviews provided complementary evidence.

The survey identified patterns across participants, while interviews provided deeper context.

## Research Convergence

Several themes appeared in both research methods, increasing confidence in their importance.

## Evidence-Based Opportunities

The research produced concrete design opportunities rather than only general observations.

## Documentation Structure

The project established a dedicated documentation area for preserving project reasoning.

## Repository Cleanup

The public repository was eventually reorganised to contain intentional project artifacts rather than raw research and assignment-only material.

---

# 8. What Did Not Work as Well

## Documentation Was Not Maintained Consistently

Several planning documents were created as placeholders rather than being maintained throughout the actual project.

This created a gap between the work that was completed and the documentation describing how it happened.

### Lesson

Important project decisions should be recorded when they occur rather than reconstructed later whenever possible.

---

## Observation Could Not Be Conducted

Observation was not appropriate for this project because of the privacy and ethical sensitivity of financial applications.

### Lesson

Research methods need to be adapted to the sensitivity of the problem domain.

Survey and interviews provided alternative sources of evidence.

---

## Repository Scope Was Initially Too Broad

The project initially included files that were later determined to be inappropriate for the public repository.

Raw research data also existed in earlier Git history and required history cleanup.

### Lesson

Repository boundaries and data-handling decisions should be established before committing project files.

---

## Assignment and Portfolio Goals Needed Separation

The original assignment provided a useful structure, but treating the assignment as the entire project would have limited the depth of the portfolio case study.

### Lesson

Assignment requirements should be treated as constraints or starting requirements, while the portfolio should demonstrate deeper process and reasoning where appropriate.

---

# 9. Git/GitHub Lessons

The project provided practical Git learning around:

- Staging.
- Commits.
- Branches.
- Merging.
- Reset.
- Revert.
- Restore.
- `.gitignore`.
- Removing files from tracking.
- Remote tracking.
- History rewriting.
- `git-filter-repo`.
- `git push --force-with-lease`.

A key lesson was:

> Deleting a file in a later commit does not remove it from previous Git history.

The project therefore required a history rewrite after discovering that raw research data had existed in earlier commits.

The cleaned repository is now synchronized with `origin/main`.

---

# 10. Research Data Handling Lesson

The raw survey and interview response files remain available locally but are not part of the public GitHub repository.

The public repository uses summarized research findings instead.

This approach keeps the portfolio repository cleaner and reduces unnecessary exposure of participant research data.

---

# 11. What Should Be Improved Next Time

### Research Planning

Document research objectives, method selection, participant criteria, and limitations before execution.

### Decision Logging

Record significant decisions at the time they are made.

### Timeline

Maintain a simple project timeline throughout the project.

### Evidence Collection

Capture important evidence as the project progresses rather than reconstructing it later.

### Repository Planning

Define public vs private project materials before committing files.

### Research-to-Design Traceability

For each major design decision, explicitly record:

Research finding → user need → design opportunity → design decision → validation result.

---

# 12. Portfolio Lessons

The project demonstrates several areas of professional growth:

- Moving from assumptions toward evidence.
- Combining quantitative and qualitative research.
- Handling research limitations honestly.
- Translating research findings into design opportunities.
- Learning practical Git/GitHub workflows.
- Making deliberate decisions about what belongs in a public repository.
- Recognising the importance of documenting the reasoning behind design work.

The remaining design phase should preserve this evidence-based approach.

The immediate next phase is:

4. User journeys and key flows.
5. Information architecture.

---

# 14. Retrospective Summary

The most important lesson from the project so far is that a UX case study is not only the final interface.

The value of the LoanEase project comes from showing:

**Problem → Assumptions → Research → Evidence → Synthesis → Design → Testing → Iteration → Learning**

The remaining project work should preserve that chain and make the reasoning visible.
